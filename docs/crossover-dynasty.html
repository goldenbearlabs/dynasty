Crossover Dynasty

A multi-sport dynasty league where one franchise owns rosters, reserve lists and draft picks in every sport, a high school recruit can stay with you through college and into the NBA, and one trade can move an NBA first-rounder for an NHL goaltender. Go, Postgres, SvelteKit, one small server.

01The core idea

Four entities carry the design:

  • Dynasty: your friend group's whole universe. Created once in a setup wizard, where you pick which sports to include (any subset of CBB, NBA, NHL, NFL, MLB) and the cross-sport rules.
  • League: one per chosen sport, inside the dynasty. It lives for years and has a new season each year. All of its rules sit in one settings document.
  • Franchise: one per manager, spanning every league in the dynasty.
  • Asset: a rostered player or a draft pick. Each belongs to one league and one franchise.

A cross-league trade needs no special machinery. A trade is a list of asset transfers, and each transfer names its own league. When they all share a league it is an ordinary trade; when they don't, it is a cross-sport trade. Same table, same code path, same database transaction.

02Keeping it lean

You asked for clean, reusable and readable, and you also asked for nearly everything to be customizable. Those two pull against each other unless the code follows a few rules. These are the ones the rest of the plan is built on.

04Players & continuity

One person is one row in players, for their whole career. The row carries a competition (where he plays, or is headed) and a status of prospect, active or inactive. A high school recruit is a CBB prospect. A European teenager is an NBA prospect. An NHL draftee still in junior is an NHL prospect.

This works for basketball because of something I confirmed on the live feeds: ESPN keeps the same athlete id from college into the NBA. Cooper Flagg is 5041939 in both. When the NBA fetcher sees an id that already exists as a CBB player, it updates that row's competition to nba. No name matching is needed for that step.

Who is in each seasonal draft

The pool is always “plays in this competition, on nobody's roster in this league.” What differs per sport is only who has recently entered the pool:

LeagueNew entrants each yearWhere they come from
CBBHigh school recruits, plus any unrostered college player (transfers included)ESPN recruiting feed (385 players in the 2027 class), with manual entry as backup
NBAInternational players and unrostered NBA players. CBB graduates only when continuity is offESPN NBA draft feed for drafted internationals; manual entry for the rest
NHLDraft picks not yet in the league, players ranked for the next draft, and unrostered playersThe last five draft classes from the NHL's records (with permanent player ids), Central Scouting's rankings for the next draft once published, and each club's own prospect list
NFLReal rookies and unrostered playersESPN NFL draft feed and team rosters
MLBReal draft picks and unrostered playersMLB draft feed (ids carry through to the majors)

Three admin tools that make this workable

  • Add players. A form for one player and a paste-in CSV for a list. This covers anyone the feeds miss: an unranked recruit, a 17-year-old in Spain.
  • Merge two rows. Two sources use a different id before and after a player arrives: ESPN recruits, and anything entered by hand. (NHL prospects and MLB draft picks keep one id for life, and NFL and NBA rookies simply appear on rosters.) When the real player appears, the admin page suggests matches by name and the commissioner confirms. Merging moves the roster entry, picks and stats onto one row.
  • Review queue. Any rostered player who has dropped out of his competition's feed is listed for the commissioner: a college player who left without reaching the NBA, a retirement, a release overseas.

Open free agency needs one guard

With open free agency, someone could add the real-life first overall pick the minute he appears in the feed, and the seasonal draft would be pointless. So the plan includes a default rule: a player who entered the pool after the league's last draft can only be acquired in the next draft. After that draft, anyone left undrafted becomes a normal free agent. It is one date comparison (players.eligible_since against the last draft's completion time) and one setting to turn it off.

ESPN's endpoints are undocumented

Three of the five sports depend on endpoints ESPN has never promised to keep stable. Two cheap defenses belong in the first ingestion milestone: keep each provider behind its own small adapter package, and save the latest raw response for each URL to a raw_payloads table before parsing it. When a feed changes shape you can see exactly what it returned and re-run the sync after fixing the parser.

06Schema

About twenty tables in four groups. Columns that hold a key design decision are highlighted.

Dynasty, leagues, franchises

create table dynasties (
  id        uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  name      text not null,
  settings  jsonb not null              -- sports chosen, overall title
);

create table franchises (
  id            uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  dynasty_id    uuid not null references dynasties(id),
  name          text not null,
  manager_name  text not null,
  slug          text not null,
  invite_token  text not null unique,   -- personal sign-in link
  is_commissioner boolean not null default false,
  unique (dynasty_id, slug)
);

  id          uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  dynasty_id  uuid not null references dynasties(id),
  competition text not null,            -- registry key: cbb|nba|nhl|nfl|mlb

);

create table seasons (
  id          uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),

  year        int  not null,
  starts_on   date not null, ends_on date not null,
  status      text not null default 'upcoming', -- upcoming|active|complete
  champion_franchise_id uuid references franchises(id),
  unique (league_id, year)
);

Real-world data

Drafts, trades, history

create table drafts (
  id          uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  dynasty_id  uuid not null references dynasties(id),
  kind        text not null,            -- startup|seasonal
  year        int  not null,
  status      text not null default 'scheduled', -- scheduled|live|paused|complete
  clock_expires_at timestamptz,         -- stored, so a restart never loses the clock

-- one league for a normal draft; several for a combined startup draft
create table draft_leagues (
  draft_id  uuid references drafts(id),
  league_id uuid references leagues(id),
  primary key (draft_id, league_id)
);

-- A pick is a tradeable asset before it is used and a record after.
create table draft_picks (
  id           uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  draft_id     uuid not null references drafts(id),
  round        int  not null,
  position     int,                     -- overall order; null until the commissioner sets it
  original_franchise_id uuid not null references franchises(id),
  current_franchise_id  uuid not null references franchises(id),
  player_id    uuid references players(id),  -- filled when the pick is made
  picked_at    timestamptz,
  auto_picked  boolean not null default false
);

create table trades (
  id          uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  dynasty_id  uuid not null references dynasties(id),
  status      text not null default 'proposed',
              -- proposed|accepted|rejected|cancelled|executed|reversed
  proposed_by uuid not null references franchises(id),
  note        text,
  created_at  timestamptz not null default now(),
  resolved_at timestamptz
);

create table trade_parties (          -- a separate table, so three-way trades work
  trade_id     uuid references trades(id) on delete cascade,
  franchise_id uuid references franchises(id),
  accepted_at  timestamptz,
  primary key (trade_id, franchise_id)
);

create table trade_items (
  id             uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  trade_id       uuid not null references trades(id) on delete cascade,
  from_franchise uuid not null references franchises(id),
  to_franchise   uuid not null references franchises(id),
  league_id      uuid references leagues(id),   -- each player item names its own league
  player_id      uuid references players(id),
  draft_pick_id  uuid references draft_picks(id),
  check (num_nonnulls(player_id, draft_pick_id) = 1)
);

-- Append-only: the activity feed and the audit trail.
create table transactions (
  id           bigserial primary key,
  dynasty_id   uuid not null references dynasties(id),
  league_id    uuid references leagues(id),
  franchise_id uuid references franchises(id),
  kind         text not null,  -- add|drop|pick|trade|graduated|move_list|settings|commissioner
  player_id    uuid, draft_pick_id uuid, trade_id uuid,
  detail       jsonb,
  created_at   timestamptz not null default now()
);

-- plus two small ingestion tables: ingest_runs and raw_payloads

Keeping original_franchise_id beside current_franchise_id is what lets the page say “2028 NHL 1st (via Sam).” Future seasonal drafts are created ahead of time as scheduled rows, as many years out as the league's future_years setting, so future picks exist and can be traded.

07Rosters & lineups

Main roster and reserve list

Each franchise has a main roster and a reserve list in every league, with sizes from settings. Only main-roster players can be put in a starting lineup. Reserve players never score. A manager can move a player between the two lists at any time, subject to the sizes and the reserve eligibility rule.

One check for every roster move

roster.Check(league, franchise, change) answers one question: after this change, is the franchise within its main and reserve limits, and is each reserve player eligible to be there? Free agent adds, draft picks, trades, list moves and graduations all call it.

One exception is deliberate. A CBB graduation, or the commissioner shrinking roster sizes, can push a franchise over a limit through no action of its own. Those changes go through, and the franchise is marked over the limit. While over, it can only make moves that reduce the overage.

Free agency

When a league's free agency is open, any manager can add an available player at any time, first come first served, if roster.Check passes. Drops are always allowed. Adds pause while that league's draft is live, so the draft pool cannot change under the room. New entrants wait for the next draft, as described in section 04.

Lineups

  • The league's slots setting defines the starting positions. A player fits a slot if one of his positions is in the slot's list.
  • A lineup is saved with an effective date and stays in force until a newer one is saved. Nobody has to re-enter a lineup every day, and no nightly copy job is needed.
  • Daily leagues can change the lineup for any future date. Weekly leagues can only change it from the start of a scoring period.
  • Locks come from games.starts_at. With game_start locking, a player cannot be moved in or out once his game that day (or his first game that week) has started. The commissioner can override a lock.
  • A sports day follows US Eastern time and turns over at 5am, so a late West Coast or Hawaii game counts for the evening it is played on.
  • A player who is dropped, traded or moved to reserve leaves the lineup from that day on. If his game that day has already started, the change takes effect the next day, so points already scored stand.

08Drafts

Startup and seasonal drafts run on the same code. The only real difference is who is already on a roster when the draft begins: nobody at startup, most players in later years.

Per-sport draft

The draft covers one league. Used for every seasonal draft, and for startup if you draft each sport separately.

Combined startup draft

The draft covers several leagues at once. Any pick can be spent on a player from any included sport, and the pick lands him on that sport's roster. In the code this is the same draft with more than one row in draft_leagues; the availability rule and roster.Check already work per league.

Commissioner controls

  • Generate picks from rounds and an order (linear or snake).
  • Then edit freely before the draft starts: reassign any pick, reorder, add or remove picks.
  • Start, pause, resume, undo the last pick, pick on someone's behalf.
  • Set the pick clock, or 0 for no clock.

How the room works

  • The server holds the truth. Each pick is written to the database first, then announced to everyone over a websocket.
  • The clock is a stored deadline, not a countdown in memory. A two-minute live draft and an eight-hours-per-pick slow draft are the same feature with a different number, and both survive a server restart.
  • When the clock expires the server picks the first available player from that manager's queue. With an empty queue the pick is skipped, and the manager can make it up at any time while the draft is live.
  • Free agency in a league is paused while its draft is running, and when a draft completes, everyone it passed over becomes a free agent.
  • When picking, the manager chooses main roster or reserve for the player, if he is eligible for reserve.
  • A browser that reconnects receives the full current state, then live updates.

09Trades

  1. Check each affected league. A trade touching NBA and NHL must leave both franchises legal in both leagues. The engine runs roster.Check once per league and franchise, and a rejection names the league that failed.
  2. Execute in one database transaction and re-check inside it. Ownership can change between proposal and acceptance. Inside the transaction, lock every roster entry and pick involved, confirm each still belongs to its sender, then move everything and write the log rows. Any failure undoes all of it.
  3. Assets move by update. The only changes are roster_entries.franchise_id and draft_picks.current_franchise_id. A traded starter is removed from the old franchise's future lineups.

Roster limits are checked when the last party accepts, not when the offer is made, so a manager can receive an offer first and make room second. A trade is proposed, becomes executed when every party has accepted (and the commissioner has approved, if that setting is on), or ends as rejected or cancelled. Picks can be traded only until their draft starts. The commissioner can reverse an executed trade, which runs the same engine with the items flipped. There is no valuation or fairness logic anywhere.

10Scoring & titles

Each fetcher converts its box score into the same shape: a set of canonical stat keys per player per game. NBA's "12-22" becomes fgm: 12, fga: 22; NHL's goalie line becomes saves and goals_against. From there a single query scores every sport:

-- fantasy points per franchise per day, for any sport
select l.franchise_id, g.starts_at::date as day,
       sum(s.value::numeric * rule.value::numeric) as points
from   active_lineups l                       -- view: the lineup in force on each date
join   games g       on g.starts_at::date = l.day
join   stat_lines sl on sl.game_id = g.id and sl.player_id = l.player_id
join   leagues lg    on lg.id = l.league_id
cross join lateral jsonb_each_text(sl.stats) s
join lateral jsonb_each_text(lg.settings->'scoring') rule on rule.key = s.key
group by 1, 2;

At ten franchises this is fast enough to run on every page load. If it ever is not, the same query can fill a cache table without changing anything else.

Total points format

Standings are the sum of daily points over the season. No schedule to generate. The leader when the season closes is the champion.

Head-to-head format

A round-robin schedule is generated into matchups when the season starts. Each period's result comes from the two franchises' period totals. The top playoff_teams enter a bracket whose matchups are added as each round finishes.

The two formats share stat ingestion, lineups and the scoring query. They differ only in one Standings function and the schedule generator.

Live scores

  • One loop on the server polls the feeds every 30 seconds, only for sports the dynasty plays and only while a game is live or due to start. With nothing in play it makes no requests.
  • Each poll is one scoreboard request per sport plus one box score per live game. The result is written to the database first.
  • Browsers hold a websocket per sport's scoreboard or per game page and receive each change as it is stored. No browser polls, so the load on the feeds is the same for one viewer or twenty.
  • Pages that show fantasy points (standings, matchups, the lineup page) listen on the same connection and re-read their totals when a score changes, so points are still computed in one place.

Head-to-head details

  • The schedule is a round robin of matchup_days-long periods; with an odd number of franchises one has a bye each period.
  • Standings rank by share of matchups won (a tie counts half), then by points.
  • The last periods of the season are the playoffs. Each round is set when the one before it ends, re-seeded so the best remaining seed plays the worst; the better seed wins a tie.
  • The winner of the final is the default champion when the commissioner closes the season.

Titles

  • Five league titles. When a season ends the system proposes the champion and the commissioner confirms, which writes seasons.champion_franchise_id.
  • Overall title, optional. Each franchise earns points for its finishing place in each league (the points_by_finish list), summed across all leagues for that year. It is computed from the season results; nothing extra is stored.

13Auth & deployment

Accounts

Each manager has an account: an email and a password. The commissioner creates theirs during setup. Everyone else gets an invite link for their franchise; opening it lets them choose their email and password, and the link then stops working. A forgotten password is fixed the same way: the commissioner issues a new link, which also signs out anywhere the old password was used. No email is ever sent.

Signing in issues a JWT, kept in a cookie that page scripts cannot read. It lasts 30 days and is renewed on each visit, so a manager who comes back is taken straight to their team. The signing key is generated on first start and stored in the database, so there is nothing to configure.

The authorization rule is unchanged: every request that changes something checks that the signed-in franchise owns the thing being changed, or is a commissioner. Open trade offers are visible only to their parties and commissioners.

15Open questions

Each of these has a default written into the plan. They are listed so you can overrule any of them before the code depends on it.

QuestionDefault in this plan
With open free agency, can a brand-new rookie or recruit be added before the seasonal draft?No. New entrants can only be drafted; undrafted ones become free agents afterwards. One setting turns this off.
Who may sit on the reserve list?Prospects only: players who have not yet reached the competition's top level. The alternative setting is “anyone.”
Where does a CBB graduate land in the NBA league?On the franchise's NBA reserve list, even if that puts it over the limit; the franchise must then get back under before making other moves.
What about a rostered CBB player who leaves college but does not reach the NBA?He appears in the commissioner's review queue. The commissioner either carries him over as an NBA prospect or releases him.
Does every franchise play in every league of the dynasty?Yes. Nobody sits a sport out.
How is the overall title scored, and which seasons count as one year?Points by finishing place in each league, summed. Seasons are grouped by the year they start in (2026 NFL with 2026–27 NBA).
What happens when a draft clock runs out and the manager's queue is empty?The pick is skipped and can be made later. No automatic “best available,” since that would need a ranking and you ruled out valuation logic.
Is there a limit on free agent adds, or a waiting period after a drop?No limits. A dropped player is available to everyone at once.
Will there ever be more than one dynasty on the server?The schema allows it, but the first version's screens assume one.

Not planned

Trade valuation or fairness scoring, waiver priority or bidding, push notifications, a mobile app, real accounts with passwords, and public league pages.
